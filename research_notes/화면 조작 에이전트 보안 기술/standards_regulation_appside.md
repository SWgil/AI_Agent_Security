## 1. Frameworks and standards that apply to GUI/screen-operating agents

### Takeaway
By Nov 2026 there is a dense layer of voluntary agent-security taxonomies (OWASP ASI Top 10, MITRE ATLAS agent techniques, NIST/CAISI initiative) but none is specific to screen operation; the only standards that directly regulate "reading and clicking another app's GUI" are Chinese (a financial-sector group standard requiring institution authorization, plus a mandatory national standard in preparation). Korea has drafts and plans (PIPC agentic-AI guide due end-2026; FSI agent red-team criteria in pilot), not final rules.

### Inferences
- The international frameworks treat GUI agents as one instance of "agent with tools"; the screen-specific risks (visual prompt injection, bystander data on screen, operating third-party apps without their consent) are covered only indirectly through ASI01/ASI02-type categories. China is the one jurisdiction that has turned "the operated app must also consent" into written norms.
- Korea's approach so far is privacy-led (PIPC) and finance-sector red-teaming (FSI); nothing found resembles China's "dual authorization" rule or a KISA agent-specific guide.

## 2. Regulation and liability when an agent acts on the user's behalf

### Takeaway
Liability for an agent's mistaken or injected action is unsettled everywhere: the one appellate ruling (Ninth Circuit, Aug 2026, Amazon v. Perplexity) treated the agent as the user's tool for computer-access law but expressly declined to set broad principles, payment law (Reg E/Z, PSD2/PSR) still assumes a human payer, and vendor terms push responsibility for agent-initiated transactions onto the user.

### Cited Findings

**Amazon v. Perplexity (Comet)**
- Amazon sued Perplexity in Nov 2025 under the federal CFAA and California's CDAFA over Comet's agent logging in and shopping in users' Amazon accounts. — [Retail Insight Network, Aug 2026](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/newsletters/us-court-overturns-amazon-injunction-perplexity)
- Mar 2026 (reports give 6, 9 or 10 Mar): Judge Maxine Chesney (N.D. Cal.) granted a preliminary injunction barring Comet's agent from password-protected parts of Amazon, finding access "with the Amazon user's permission, but without authorization by Amazon"; denied Perplexity's request for a US$1bn bond; stayed the order 7 days for appeal. [search summary] — [GeekWire](https://geekwire.com/2026/judge-blocks-perplexitys-ai-bot-from-shopping-on-amazon-in-early-test-of-agentic-commerce/), [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bgov.com/ip-law/amazon-wins-court-order-blocking-perplexitys-ai-shopping-bots), [The Fashion Law](https://www.thefashionlaw.com/court-blocks-perplexity-from-accessing-amazon-systems-in-agentic-ai-lawsuit/)
- Aug 2026 (article dated 5 Aug 2026; ruling date not stated): a three-judge Ninth Circuit panel, which had stayed the injunction pending appeal, vacated it. It found Amazon unlikely to succeed on CFAA and CDAFA because, on the current record, users — not Perplexity — access Amazon: "Perplexity's AI Assistant operates as a tool acting on the user's instructions." Other injunction factors favoured Perplexity. The panel limited the ruling to the record and said it "does not set broader legal principles regarding agentic AI". Case returns to N.D. Cal. — [Retail Insight Network](https://www.retail-insight-network.com/newsletters/us-court-overturns-amazon-injunction-perplexity)
- Amazon said it disagreed and was evaluating options (rehearing or Supreme Court review possible); the underlying suit continues. [search summary] — [eWeek](https://www.eweek.com/news/perplexity-ai-shopping-agent-returns-amazon/)

**United States — payment and consumer law**
- Regulation E (12 CFR 1005.2(m)) defines an unauthorized transfer as one initiated by someone other than the consumer "without actual authority" and with no benefit to the consumer; Regulation Z's card test includes "implied or apparent authority". Commentators report no CFPB rule/guidance and no court decision on whether a hijacked agent still has "actual authority"; a third category — "authorized access, unintended transaction" — is not represented. [search summary] — [SuperMoney](https://www.supermoney.com/ai-agent-unauthorized-payment-liability), [JD Supra](https://www.jdsupra.com/legalnews/when-ai-clicks-pay-the-emerging-4898967/)
- ITIF (23 Mar 2026) urged the CFPB to act, arguing Reg E as written would leave consumers without dispute rights for agentic purchases. [search summary] — [ITIF](https://itif.org/publications/2026/03/23/agentic-commerce-is-coming-but-regulation-meant-for-humans-will-slow-it-down/)
- US banks are reported to be detecting AI agents active in customer accounts and blocking access, objecting to customers handing login credentials to AI; "read-only" agent permissions (view balances/transactions, no execution) are under consideration. [search summary] — [Malwarebytes](https://www.malwarebytes.com/?p=481980)

**Vendor and merchant terms**
- OpenAI: an analysis reports that OpenAI's Service Terms (Section 14) make users responsible for transactions made on their behalf by agentic features, and that OpenAI directs most payment/order disputes to merchants; the terms cannot override statutory or cardholder rights. Primary terms not read. **Korea**

### Inferences
- The Ninth Circuit's "tool of the user" framing cuts both ways: it weakens a platform's computer-access claim against the agent vendor, and it supports treating the agent's purchase as the user's own act — consistent with vendor and merchant terms that put the transaction on the user. Prompt-injection-caused purchases remain an untested case.
- SCA in Europe (and Korea's additional-authentication practice) means a screen-operating agent normally cannot complete payment authentication itself; the step-up is the de facto human-in-the-loop control. Tokenized agent credentials from card networks (Section 3) are the industry's route around this, not a legal change.
- Korean 전자금융거래법 (Art. 9, financial-company liability for accidents; user gross negligence such as lending or delegating an access medium shifts loss to the user) and 개인정보 보호법 (an agent vendor that uploads screenshots containing third parties' chat messages or account details is processing data of people who never consented) are obvious pressure points, but no Korean regulator statement, court case or law-firm analysis applying either statute to screen agents was found; this is the researcher's reading [background, not re-verified], not a sourced finding.

### Gaps
- The Ninth Circuit opinion itself (date, panel, case number) and current district-court status after remand were not obtained; all detail is from trade press.
- Primary terms for ChatGPT agent, Gemini agent mode / Gemini app automation, Claude in Chrome / computer use, and Samsung Galaxy AI were not read; no source quoting Anthropic's, Google's or Samsung's agent-specific liability clauses was found.
- No FTC or CFPB statement specific to agent-initiated purchases was found (commentators say none exists).
- No Commission/AI Office guidance on how the AI Act classifies general-purpose agents was found; EBA position on agent-initiated payments and SCA not found. UK position not researched.
- Korea: nothing found from 금융위/금감원 on 전자금융거래법 treatment of agent-initiated transfers or erroneous agent payments.
- Other litigation involving agents operating third-party UIs: none found beyond Amazon v. Perplexity.

## 3. How third-party apps and services defend against or accommodate agents operating their UI

### Takeaway
Apps first responded with existing risk controls (anomalous-login logouts, feature blocks), and platforms then moved from "user consent is enough" to allowlists and app-side consent: Android's Computer Control automates only a signed allowlist of apps, Google Play bars third-party apps from using the Accessibility API for autonomous action, and in China refusal lists and institution authorization are becoming the norm. Payments are being handled by a separate cryptographic identity layer (Web Bot Auth + Visa TAP / Mastercard Agent Pay) rather than by letting agents type card numbers.

### Cited Findings

**App-side blocking in practice (China)**
- Within a week of the Doubao phone (Nubia M153) going on sale in Dec 2025, Taobao, Alipay, Meituan and Pinduoduo imposed restrictions; 8 of 23 mainstream apps reportedly would not run normally. Users whose WeChat was operated by the assistant got "登录环境存在异常" pop-ups and forced logouts; Doubao withdrew WeChat operation. — [36Kr, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.36kr.com/p/3838411102534533)
- WeChat staff said no Doubao-specific measures were taken; anomalies likely came from existing risk controls triggered by automation. — [CBNData, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.cbndata.com/information/295610)
- Dec 2025: users reported bank-app login anomalies and blocked payments; on 6 Dec 2025 the assistant took financial-app operation offline. — [封面新闻, 11 Sep 2026](https://cbgc.scol.com.cn/news/7944927)
- Doubao "Screen Automation Operation Declaration Protocol" (屏幕自动化操作声明协议, SAEP): on 14 Oct 2026 Doubao reportedly published its progress and a refusal list — 150+ apps including WeChat, Meituan and Alipay declined; second-generation Doubao reportedly pairs SAEP with a 30-day public-notice period giving developers a right to refuse. Seen only in a search digest; the three pages opened did not contain it. UNVERIFIED. [search summary] — [Huxiu](https://www.huxiu.com/article/4894229.html?f=rss), [Jiemian](https://www.jiemian.com/article/14862992.html)

**Android / Google Play**
- Google Play's Accessibility API policy prohibits using the API for "an app that autonomously initiates, plans, and executes actions or decisions"; only apps whose core function directly supports people with disabilities may declare `isAccessibilityTool`; others must disclose and obtain consent. — [Play Console Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16585319) (fetched Nov 2026)

**Web: agent identity and payment authentication**
- Web Bot Auth (Cloudflare proposal, May 2025) authenticates agent traffic with HTTP Message Signatures: `Signature-Input` carries `keyid`, `alg` (Ed25519), `created`/`expires`, `nonce`, `tag`; public keys are published in a well-known directory; requests are time-bound and non-replayable. — [Cloudflare blog, 24 Oct 2025, updated 15 Jul 2026](https://blog.cloudflare.com/secure-agentic-commerce/)

### Inferences
- Three defence layers are visible: (a) app-level heuristics (risk engines, attestation, secure windows) that block agents as a side effect of anti-fraud; (b) OS/platform gatekeeping (privileged permission + signed allowlist on Android; intents-only on iOS; Play ban on accessibility-based autonomy for third parties); (c) explicit declaration schemes (China's dual authorization, Doubao's refusal list, Web Bot Auth signatures).
- The Play accessibility rule plus the privileged `ACCESS_COMPUTER_CONTROL` permission means a general-purpose third-party screen agent has no sanctioned distribution path on stock Android; screen operation is effectively reserved for the OS vendor/OEM assistant. This is a competition issue as much as a security one.
- Android's allowlist is curated by Google rather than opted into via a public developer flag (none documented); app developers' leverage is therefore indirect (FLAG_SECURE, risk controls, or negotiation).

## 4. Enterprise angle: MDM/EMM, Knox, DLP, audit

### Takeaway

## 5. Trend: structured agent interfaces in exchange for control, and the business conflicts behind it

### Takeaway
Yes — during 2026 the centre of gravity moved from uninvited GUI operation to app-granted structured interfaces (MCP, A2A, AppFunctions, App Intents), with GUI operation surviving mainly where the OS vendor curates an allowlist. The driver is as much control of the user entry point, traffic and risk management as security.

### Cited Findings
- Second-generation Doubao phone no longer reads the screen and simulates clicks after user authorization; apps must provide MCP services and permit control. Dozens of ByteDance apps support protocol calls; which outside super-apps have opened is undisclosed. — [封面新闻, 11 Sep 2026](https://cbgc.scol.com.cn/news/7944927), [CBNData, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.cbndata.com/information/295610)
- StepFun's STEPX Neo uses a "GUI-MCP" protocol in which the app decides what to expose. — [封面新闻, 11 Sep 2026](https://cbgc.scol.com.cn/news/7944927)
- WeChat partnered with Huawei, Honor, Xiaomi, OPPO and vivo on A2A assistant capability (Honor first, Jun 2026): only sending messages, voice calls and video calls are exposed; the phone assistant sends a structured instruction and WeChat executes it itself, retaining execution and risk-control authority. — [36Kr, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.36kr.com/p/3838411102534533), [封面新闻](https://cbgc.scol.com.cn/news/7944927)
- Alipay routes agent tasks to its own agent rather than letting an outside agent drive its UI. — [CBNData, 4 Aug 2026](https://www.cbndata.com/information/295610)
- Android: AppFunctions replace simulated screen control with declared, structured operations the OS can identify and restrict; Android 17 adds runtime registration, activity-scoped functions and finer access/discovery controls; Play policy for AppFunctions applies from 14 Apr 2027. One commentary notes most agents remain outside the AppFunctions "cage". ### Inferences
- App providers trade a narrow, structured capability set (WeChat: three actions) for keeping execution, risk control, advertising surface and customer relationship. GUI operation remains the fallback for the long tail of apps with no agent interface, so security technology for screen agents is still needed, but under platform-curated allowlists and app-consent regimes rather than open access.
- Outcomes diverge by legal setting: in the US the appellate court (provisionally) sided with user-authorized agents; in China standards side with app/institution consent. Korea has not yet chosen.
